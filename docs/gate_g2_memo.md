# Gate G2 assessment

**Decision — PIVOT to uncapped upper-quantile certification with an extensive-margin constraint.** The inherited sequence starting at 5% deployment missed the focal Gate G2 threshold. The prospectively specified 15%-first sensitivity certified 40.875% mean deployment for uncapped quantile risk at the same focal calibration size, against 0% for each two-arm comparator, with zero unsafe certifications in 200 draws. Capped CVaR reached 0.40% under this sensitivity. Phase 3 should test the quantile and extensive-margin intersection using an order fixed before calibration. The pilot evaluated each tail metric separately, so it has yet to establish power for the joint constraint.

## Decision rule and implementation

The primary test uses the Gate Register and WP-2.2 threshold of at least 25% mean certified deployment at $n_{\mathrm{cal}}=5{,}000$, $\beta=0.90$, $P(S^*)=0.35$, with empirical FWER at most 6% and two-arm OPRA deployment at most 5% or a deployment advantage of at least 20 percentage points. The executive section's stricter 30% target is reported separately. A quantile-only pass triggers the planned pivot to uncapped quantile and extensive-margin safety. We also report the exact separate-policy betting comparator because single-policy CDF bands can be especially wide in the upper tail.

The main fixed sequence offers 5%, 10%, …, 100% deployment, stopping at the first threshold that fails its test. All methods use the same observations, known $e(1\mid x)=0.25+0.50x$, $\varepsilon=0$, and $\delta=0.05$. The pre-run sensitivity starts the same sequence at 15% in the focal design and the paired near-null design. It checks whether the first candidate limits power. The baseline potential loss has a 40% zero mass, with positive log-normal severity. The intervention improves loss in the first priority region $X\le P(S^*)$ and worsens loss beyond it. The exact largest safe policy can include part of the adverse region because the constraint is population-marginal.

The baseline VaR interval and the localized tests use the same calibration sample in two stages. The test checks every pivot in the interval, which makes the probability bound valid without a further data split. Localized empirical Bernstein computes the interval check using exact breakpoints. Localized betting uses the same full interval and tests at fixed nulls with aGRAPA-style predictable fractions, using deterministic cell envelopes for capped CVaR. A monotone fixed-stake mixture supplies the inverted baseline VaR intervals and the separate-policy betting comparator. The direct oracle-VaR runs diagnose the effect of unknown reference quantiles. The two-arm betting CVaR row supplies both population VaRs as a diagnostic. The two-arm betting quantile row is deployable with known propensities. The OPRA baseline uses Huang et al.'s clipped inverse-propensity CDF with their tighter deterministic bound and computes tail-functional bounds by inversion or integration of the separate envelopes. [Formalization](phase2_formalization.md), [independent proof check](phase2_independent_check.md), [OPRA primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.08977v2).

The inherited code used bisection on adaptive betting tests without a monotonicity proof, treated all-zero *observed* switch weights as structural equality, and counted only unsafe final selections. The corrected run uses monotone inversion, retains the test for all-zero observed weights, and counts any certified unsafe candidate in the fixed-sequence prefix. The CVaR grid endpoint was also hardened for arbitrary tied outcomes. This final edge-case repair concerns positive internal grid knots, which an observed continuous log-normal loss hits with probability zero in the pilot. Deterministic checks covered those ties, the cap endpoint, an empty empirical switch sample, and monotone betting inversion.

At the focal setting, the exact baseline $Q_{0.90}$ is 11.27185 and capped CVaR is 18.99943. At 5% deployment the signed survival contrast at baseline VaR is $-0.004526$, and the sufficient capped-CVaR contrast is $-0.3684$. At 15% deployment the respective contrasts are $-0.013577$ and $-1.1053$. The means are exactly three times larger in magnitude because the first 15% all share the same conditional arm laws. The order sensitivity can therefore distinguish a weak first test from failure of the certificate across useful deployment levels.

At the focal size the quantile result starting at 15% exceeds both the 25% gate threshold and the 30% executive target, with 99% nontrivial runs and Monte Carlo standard error of 0.359 percentage points. The corresponding 5%-first method certified a nontrivial policy in only 18% of draws. The 15%-first comparison holds the sample, risk, two-arm controls, and testing level fixed. At 10,000 observations, quantile betting clears the 10% non-vacuity threshold even under the inherited order. Across the three $\beta$ levels with $P(S^*)=0.35$ at $n=5{,}000$, the inherited-order quantile betting means were 28.65%, 7.575%, and 0% as the target moved outward. At the focal 5%-first setting, capped CVaR remained at zero across all three cap sensitivities ($B \approx 30.23,60.46,151.15$). With a larger calibration sample and the 15%-first order, CVaR did improve, though that does not meet the focal-size rule.

For deployable methods, no focal setting in the table certified any unsafe policy in the tested prefix. The one-sided 95% exact binomial upper endpoint for 0/200 is 1.49%. In the deliberately unsafe 15%-first near-null design, the localized quantile betting false-certification rate was 0/200 at 5,000 and 3/200 (1.5%) at 10,000. The largest observed FWER for deployable localized betting anywhere in the pilot was 1.5%. The largest oracle-diagnostic rate was 5.5%. The finite-sample theorem supplies the error bound, and these empirical rates check its implementation in this design.

The larger starting mass raises the margin while retaining the variance benefit from using the signed contrast. This is why the same predeclared starting point needs to be tested on nonoracle scores in Phase 3 and later. The result supports a moderate-tail quantile target and identifies limited power for CVaR at the focal sample size.

## Run verification

The pilot ran all 27 main design cells, three strict near-null cells, three cap sensitivities, and four order sensitivities (37 cells with 200 draws each). It produced 370 risk-by-method rows, including all 54 primary risk-by-design cells, with no missing values. Each design shared identical data across methods, with separate order and cap checks paired on the same seeds where applicable. NumPy 2.5.5, SciPy 1.18.1 and pandas 3.1.1 supplied the numerical environment. The run took 330.72 seconds with two single-threaded workers. The systemd scope enforced a 4 GiB aggregate hard cap, with 143.5 MiB charged peak. The sum of process peak RSS values was bounded by 383.2 MiB. ## Primary-source and applied-data checks

The primary-source audit found no complete baseline-quantile comparative certificate among the papers examined. Jeunen (2026) already uses the signed importance-weight cancellation for mean outcomes, and Bourigault and Bourigault (2026) already give selection-valid off-policy quantile certification at an external loss budget. Huang et al. (2022) already use complementary-CDF concentration. The surviving novelty claim is limited to the comparative nonlinear-tail method as a whole. [Theorem-level source audit](phase2_e3_prior_art_check.md).

Local MEPS sample size and marginal tail summaries were reproduced. OHIE access and enough published survey rows look plausible, but the exact number of eligible households, pooled outcome distribution, and validity of the calibration law remain unresolved. The household lottery and survey selection require a design argument beyond a household split. [Read-only feasibility audit](phase5_data_feasibility.md).
