# WP-5.0 read-only data feasibility

The Oregon Health Insurance Experiment (OHIE) has a public-use archive with access terms and a plausible number of survey observations. Its exact calibration sample and assignment design are unresolved. The local MEPS HC-251 file provides a verified secondary observational sample with heavy-tailed spending. No fresh treatment-by-outcome joins, outcome regressions, or policy tuning were used in this audit.

## OHIE

The official NBER archive required interactive access in the earlier session. The Harvard Dataverse listing identifies the replication data as public and lists a separate download agreement under custom dataset terms. The archive is absent from the local project. The earlier machine summary's CC0 label and exact legal details are unverified. Use the published archive agreement to determine permitted use. [NBER data page](https://www.nber.org/research/data/oregon-health-insurance-experiment-data), [Harvard Dataverse](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/SJG1ED).

Finkelstein et al. report 74,922 individuals in the main administrative-data sample, 23,741 survey participants in Table VIII, and 24,012 survey participants in Figure I's expenditure analysis. These counts make the raw sample size feasible, pending the exact public-use field, missingness, and household counts. The published control-arm rate of positive out-of-pocket spending is 55.5%, yielding a control zero-mass proxy of 44.5%. The pooled nonmissing count for `cost_tot_oop_12m`, its pooled zero fraction and right-tail quantiles, the exact unique household count, and the number of eligible calibration units await inspection of the archive. The survey instrument references a six-month expense period despite the 12-month survey label. [Finkelstein et al., Sections III–V, Table VIII and Figure I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3535298/), [12-month survey instrument](https://www.nber.org/sites/default/files/2020-02/mail-survey-twelve-month.pdf).

The lottery sampled individuals from the signup list and assigned lottery offers to households when any member was selected. The analysis adjusts for the number of household members listed (`numhh_list`) and clusters by household. Survey analyses also include wave, the household-size-by-wave interaction, and survey weights. The target assignment is the lottery offer (intent to treat). The exact assignment probabilities for the relevant analysis frame must be obtained from the design. Empirical fractions by stratum will not establish these probabilities. Independent household splitting prevents train/calibration household overlap, but fixed lottery draw counts can induce dependence across households and survey participation can alter the target. A household-based design argument or other valid calibration law is required before making the Phase 2 i.i.d. certificate claim for OHIE. [Finkelstein et al., Section IV.B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3535298/).

**OHIE decision.** Availability is provisionally supported. The full WP-5.0 OHIE pass is pending local access, confirmation of at least 6,000 eligible independent units or an appropriate design-specific replacement, exact assignment or an appropriately justified analysis law, and confirmation of a pooled zero fraction below 0.85. The inherited JSON retains earlier estimates, including an approximate cost-variable sample size. These estimates do not establish the pass conditions.

National population analyses require `PERWT23F` and the survey design. The local analytical filter and survey inclusion rules determine the target population. AHRQ limits the public-use data to statistical reporting and analysis, with prohibitions on identification and restrictions on individually identifiable linkage. [HC-251 data-use and weighting documentation](https://meps.ahrq.gov/mepsweb/data_stats/download_data/pufs/h251/h251doc.shtml), [DVTEXP23 codebook](https://meps.ahrq.gov/mepsweb/data_stats/download_data_files_codebook.jsp?PUFId=H251&varName=DVTEXP23).

The prior treatment-only diagnostic reports that 96.09% of cross-fitted logistic probabilities and 97.75% of gradient-boosted probabilities lie in ([0.05,0.95]). The observed minima were 0.0094 and 0.0089. The inherited diagnostic fits were not independently reproduced in this audit. The ranges describe model predictions and give no known positive lower bound on the true insurance propensity. [Prior WP-5.0 diagnostic summary](../results/phase5_feasibility/data_audit_summary.json).

**MEPS decision.** Feasible for an observational sensitivity study. Design-mimicking simulation inputs supported by the audit include a 54.9% dental zero fraction, a marginal dental upper-tail ratio (Q_{0.99}/Q_{0.90}) of 6.42, and an overlap stress setting near 0.05 motivated by the treatment-only diagnostics. The Phase 2 known-propensity theorem does not apply to this survey as observed.
