# Independent check of Phase 0 and the Phase 2 validity argument

The existing plan documents a completed Phase 0 and a decision to proceed with Pivot A. The original claim about applying monotone conformal risk control to population CVaR needed a sharper witness. The two distributions in the Phase 0 table both have CVaR equal to 40, and so does every mixture of the pair at level 0.90. The following witness establishes the defect and preserves the original gate decision. [Phase 0 decision and assumptions](../research_plan.md#3-fatal-flaw-certificate-phase-0--gate-g0)

Let two covariate groups have equal probability, with a known randomized treatment probability of 1/2. In group one, let baseline loss be uniform on $[2,3]$ and treatment loss be zero. Reverse the losses in group two. At level 0.90, the baseline and the policy that treats both groups each have CVaR 2.9. The intermediate policy that treats only group one has CVaR 0. Therefore even a nested deployment sequence can have nonmonotone population CVaR. Also, the equally weighted mixture of a point mass at zero and a uniform law on $[2,3]$ has CVaR 2.9, whereas the corresponding average of the component CVaRs is 1.475. The calculation averages the largest 20% of the uniform component in the mixture, and the largest 10% of that component on its own. [CVaR minimization formula for general distributions](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~rtr/papers/rtr187-CVaR2.pdf)

The corrected Phase 2 validity argument holds for independent calibration units, known treatment propensities, a training-fixed sequence of candidate policies and nonnegative tolerances. Finite-sample validity and deployment power are separate findings. The argument below does not depend on simulation outcomes. [Phase 2 formalization](phase2_formalization.md)

| Claim checked | Proof steps and boundary checks | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Policy identification and localization | Condition on the covariates and sum over the randomized action. For each policy the logging propensity cancels its inverse. The difference is identically zero where the two policies agree. | Verified under A1–A5a |
| Baseline quantile coverage | The lower endpoint error implies noncoverage of the survival mean at the deterministic true quantile. The upper endpoint error implies eventual noncoverage at a deterministic sequence of thresholds approaching that quantile from below. The latter probability is bounded by Fatou's lemma. This argument covers atoms, including a quantile of zero, and permits reuse of the calibration sample in the comparison. | Verified for the pointwise bounds stated in the proof |
| Capped CVaR comparison | Evaluate the candidate stop-loss objective at a minimizing baseline quantile. Subtracting the baseline optimum gives a sufficient upper bound. The empirical Bernstein upper bound is convex between observed loss breakpoints. For betting, the fixed cell envelope bounds the score observation by observation. On quantile coverage, passing every relevant cell implies passing the test at a fixed cell containing the true quantile. | Verified for the bounded loss and both test constructions |
| Uncapped quantile and extensive margin | Quantile harm implies a positive survival contrast at the true baseline quantile, using the right-continuous CDF convention. For positive tolerance the candidate term jumps at shifted observed losses, and the baseline term jumps at unshifted losses. Including finite interval endpoints covers ties. Extensive-margin risk is the bounded contrast of event probabilities. | Verified, with switch-region cancellation for the quantile score at zero tolerance |
| Fixed-sequence error control | An unsafe certificate in the prefix requires certification of the first truly unsafe candidate. At that candidate, passing the intersection of component tests requires passing a deterministic unsafe component. Its level bound, combined with the probability that the relevant baseline quantile misses its confidence interval, controls the full prefix. The selected-policy guarantee follows even when the sequence contains a later safe candidate. | Verified without a monotone population risk assumption |
| Switch-region variance | Squaring the score eliminates cross-terms because the two compared action indicators are disjoint on the switch region. The second moment is bounded by the switch mass times $2(B-\eta)^2/\kappa$ for the stop-loss score and by the analogous conditional survival bound for the indicator score. | Verified. An extra uniform factor of $1-\beta$ requires conditional tail control in the switch region |
| Estimated propensity extension | Under the stated multiplicative ratio bound, signed score inflation dominates the true score and preserves its zero set at zero tolerance. The additive bias bound follows by conditioning on action and applying the overlap lower bound to the estimated propensity. | Verified as a sensitivity calculation. Application requires design-appropriate sampling and a justified sensitivity parameter |

Two boundary constructions explain the corrections. For the variance claim, take $X\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]$, $L(0)=X$, $L(1)=0$, treatment probability 1/2, and a switch region $X>\beta$ of mass $p=1-\beta$. Then $\eta_0^*=\beta$ and $Z^{Q,0}=-2\mathbf 1\{X>\beta,A=0\}$ has variance $2p-p^2$, which disproves the original uniform $O(p(1-\beta)/\kappa)$ assertion. For the inherited code's all-zero shortcut, let the switch region have mass 0.01, let baseline loss be zero everywhere, and let treatment loss be one on the switch region. Capped CVaR strictly increases at any $\beta\in(0,1)$. With 200 i.i.d. calibration observations there are no switch observations with probability $0.99^{200}>0.13$. Accepting on an all-zero weight vector can therefore certify an unsafe first candidate with probability above 0.05. The revised code removes that shortcut.

The source checks use Maurer and Pontil's Theorem 4 for a bounded i.i.d. score, Rockafellar and Uryasev's Theorem 10 for CVaR with atoms, and Angelopoulos et al.'s Propositions 4 and 6 for fixed-sequence and intersection–union testing. Predictability and nonnegative factors yield the one-sided wealth test by Ville's inequality. For inversion, the implementation uses fixed-stake mixtures whose wealth increases with the hypothesized mean. Monotonicity does not follow for the direct adaptive betting rule. These checks match the cited results to the local argument without re-auditing the cited proofs.
